The story in brief

Ready to edit. Every figure is in the tables below.

The median asking price of a home for sale in Great Britain was £335,000 on 21 September 2026, according to a count of 533,560 residential listings on a major property portal by data firm HLP Data. Half of all listings were priced between £219,995 and £525,000.

Bedrooms carry a clear premium. The median asking price was £240,000 for a two-bedroom home and £325,000 for three bedrooms, a step of £85,000. Four-bedroom homes had a median of £500,000.

Across 369 areas with at least 100 listings, medians ran from £129,000 in Hartlepool to £1,850,000 in Jersey.

The agency market is highly fragmented: 7,445 agency brands appeared on the portal, and the ten with the most listings accounted for 10.1% of them. On a second national portal, counted on 2 and 3 October 2026 (414,373 listings), there were 5,285 brands and the top ten held 13.1%.

Method and limits

What these figures measure and what they do not.

  • Asking prices on one portal on one date (21 September 2026), not sold prices.
  • Covers England, Scotland and Wales, in 3,121 postcode areas. Northern Ireland is not covered. A small number of Channel Islands listings carried by the portal are included, which is why Jersey appears as an area.
  • Residential sale listings with a price of at least £1,000. Land, plots, garages, parking and commercial property are left out of every median.
  • An area is listed only with at least 100 listings. Area names are the portal's own labels.
  • Agencies are counted by brand: where a portal shows a brand with a branch after a dash, the branches are grouped under the brand. Franchise offices that trade under their own name count separately. No agency is named here.
  • No floor areas or prices per square metre: the portal publishes a floor area for a small share of listings only.
  • Collected from the portal's public pages. HLP Data is not affiliated with or licensed by any portal.
  • 1,500 rows observed on another date are left out.
